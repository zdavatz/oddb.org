# RSS-Feeds

Vorfall-Historie und Regeln aus `CLAUDE.md` (ausgelagert am 02.09.2026). Neue Notizen zu diesem Teilsystem gehoeren hierher, nicht in `CLAUDE.md`.

### RSS feeds as HTML (`jobs`-generated, `src/util/rss_reader.rb`)

- `/de/gcc/rss_html/channel/<name>.rss` renders the same pre-generated feed in the site's look and feel. The feed title on the home page points there with `target="_blank"`; the feed icon still points at the XML.
- **`RssReader` reads line by line and stops at the limit** — no XML parser, deliberately: `fachinfo.rss` is **248 MB**. It answers in 0.1s. The feeds come from `RSS::Maker`, so every field sits on its own line, which is what makes this defensible. Descriptions are dropped when longer than 600 chars or when they contain a document — the price feeds carry a rendered 28 KB HTML page per item.
- **Adding an event takes three places, not one**: the method on the state, the `require`, and the name in `EVENTS` in `src/util/validator.rb`. Miss the third and `SBSM::Session#event` returns nil, no trigger happens, and you get the *previous page* back with status 200 — no error anywhere. That cost a debugging round.
- **Keep the XML.** Feedly polls `recall`, `sl_introduction`, `price_cut`, `price_rise` and `hpc` about 57 times a day each (~290 total). `fachinfo.rss` gets 2. The cost is all on the fachinfo side: `fachinfo.rss` alone is 238 MB, and `data/rss/` holds 1.3 GB. (The 1226 seconds and the 3.9 GB peak this note used to report were the *old* `update_yearly_fachinfo_feeds`; since the rewrite the yearly files take 477 s and stay flat at ~280 MB — see below.)
- **The pages page by year, and there are two shapes of history.** `fachinfo` keeps yearly files next to the big one, so a year view reads `fachinfo-2015.rss` and never touches the 248 MB file; the other channels are a single small file where the years come from the entries' own dates, read once for both the year list and the entries.
- **Three feeds have no history at all, and that is not the page's fault.** `update_price_feeds(month)` computes a one-month window and **overwrites** `sl_introduction.rss`, `price_cut.rss` and `price_rise.rss` on every run — measured: 37, 40 and **1** entry, all from the current month, while `recall.rss` reaches back to 2018 and `hpc.rss` to 2024. The data exists in the packages' price history, but recomputing it per request is an `each_package` walk over 10 000 packages per month. Real history means writing monthly archives, `sl_introduction-2026-08.rss` in the manner of `fachinfo-2015.rss`, plus one backfill.
- The entries render as a table with a header row: `recall` and `hpc` write `Swissmedic-Registration <number>` into every description, and repeating that word on every line is what a column heading is for.
- **The entry page is the newest year, not the newest month.** `update_price_feeds` overwrites `sl_introduction.rss`, `price_cut.rss` and `price_rise.rss` with a one-month window on every run, and the no-year view read exactly that file — so `/rss_html/channel/price_cut.rss` said "Juli 2026" while January to June sat beside it as monthly archives and the year chooser offered 1955 to 2026. The year views were right the whole time; only the page one lands on was wrong, which is the kind of fault that gets reported as missing data. Where monthly archives exist the entry page is now the newest year, assembled from them (price_cut 2026: 1400 entries in 0.28 s). **`fachinfo` keeps the newest-50 view** — a single year is 231 MB there — and it is the only channel that still shows the "Neueste" entry in the chooser; everywhere else that entry pointed at the page the newest year already is. Regression tests: `test/test_state/rss/html.rb`.
- **`fachinfo-2026.rss` is a legitimate yearly file, and splitting it produced `fachinfo-2026-2026-01.rss`** — 677 MB of doubled-year duplicates across the three languages. `split_rss_archives` strips a trailing `-YYYY` from the base name now, but the files from the earlier run are still on disk and are reachable by nothing: the glob for monthly archives is `<base>-YYYY-MM.rss`, which they do not match.
- **The Patinfo has the same feeds since 30.08.2026**, and the body is shared: `ODDB::YearFeeds` (`src/util/year_feeds.rb`) holds it, `FachinfoYearFeeds` and `PatinfoYearFeeds` supply the source list, the link and the channel titles. `/de/gcc/rss_html/channel/patinfo.rss` pages by year like the Fachinfo one, and the home page's RSS box links it directly under the Fachinfo entry (`View::Drugs::PatinfoNews`, count and month out of `app.rss_updates["patinfo.rss"]`). Jobs: `jobs/build_patinfo_year_feeds` (dry by default) and `jobs/update_patinfo_rss_feeds`, nightly at 05:01 behind the Fachinfo one. Adding a channel takes the ENUM in `src/util/validator.rb` too, or `_event_url(:rss, channel: ...)` produces nothing.
- **Both boxes on the home page name the count**, out of `app.rss_updates["<channel>.rss"]` — `[newest date, changes in that month]`, written by the two update jobs from `YearFeeds#latest`. Before the first run there is no number and none is shown; "0 Änderungen" would be a claim nobody measured. The dictionary keys are `:fachinfo_changes_title` / `:patinfo_changes_title` — **not** `:fachinfo_feed_title`, which already exists and is the channel title of `fachinfo.rss` (`view/rss/fachinfo.rb:87`). Reusing that name puts a second entry with the same key in the same hash literal, the last one wins, and the feed's own title changes without a word of warning.
- **The Patinfo differs in exactly one thing, and everything else follows from it: the diff's address.** `/show/patinfo/<iksnr>/<seqnr>/<ikscd>/diff/<date>` needs sequence and package (`Session::PI_DIFF_REGEXP`), while the Fachinfo needs only the registration — and a Patinfo hangs on sequences, not registrations. So the walk goes registration → sequence → package, and it walks the **live registrations** rather than `app.patinfos`: only a package that still exists yields a link the page can resolve. There is no full-text `patinfo.rss` in the shape of the 248 MB `fachinfo.rss`; `write_latest` puts the newest fifty entries beside the yearly files so the subscribe icon and `Rss::PassThru` find something.
- **`SimpleLanguage#description` falls back to the first language present** (`language.rb:33`), so `fi.fr` on a document with no French text hands back the German one. Reading `descriptions[lang]` directly is what keeps the German history out of the French feed; `YearFeeds#document_for` does that for both classes now.
- **Retiring the registration is not enough — the sequence and the package have to go too.** A sequence is also held by its ATC class and all of those sit in `app.atc_classes`, so the path through the ATC class keeps sequence, package and Patinfo alive. Measured: 205 MB after 300 registrations, 624 MB after 2000, which extrapolates past four gigabytes; with the two extra `retire` calls it stays flat for most of the run and peaks at 1.3 GB.
- **A year page is not free to render, and that is not new.** Measured against the restarted app on 30.08.2026: 30 calls to `/rss_html/channel/patinfo.rss` took it from 1437 to 1877 MB, 30 calls to the fachinfo one from 1437 to 1635 MB - about 14 MB and 6.6 MB per request, and Ruby keeps the heap it grew. The difference is mostly the file: patinfo's newest year is 2.9 MB against fachinfo's 1.9. Relevant because the backends exit past `MEMORY_LIMIT_CRAWLER` (4000 MB) and `MEMORY_LIMIT` (6144 MB); a crawler walking the year chooser is what would get there.
- **The backfill of 30.08.2026**: 33 files, 47595 changes over 6427 Patinfos, 24859 German entries and 22736 French, 19 minutes. **The history starts in 2017**, because that is when the PI change log was introduced (`67db6c50`, 22.08.2017) - nothing is missing before that, nothing was ever written. And that 2025/2026 hold 5717/8284 German entries against about 1500 a year before them is the `add_change_log_item` fault showing through: 26293 of 55993 PI items were unreachable, against 5154 of 73090 on the Fachinfo side. The yearly feeds are therefore thinner than the drugs' real history for the older years, and no job can recover that.
- **The yearly fachinfo feeds were a partition of the stock, not a history** (fixed 27.08.2026). `Plugin#update_yearly_fachinfo_feeds` built them from `app.sorted_fachinfos` — today's Fachinfos, grouped by the year of their **last** revision. Each Fachinfo has exactly one `revision` date, so each landed in exactly one year: measured, **5053 distinct Fachinfos across all yearly files and three of them in more than one year**. Old years therefore *shrink* whenever a document is touched again — February 2026's mass reparse pulled 2191 documents out of their earlier years, which is why 2025 stood at 66 entries and 2026 at 2871. A drug whose text was rewritten in 2019, 2022 and 2025 belonged in all three and appeared in none but the last.
- **The history lives in the documents' `change_log`** — the same source the `/diff/` pages read. `ODDB::FachinfoYearFeeds` (`src/util/fachinfo_year_feeds.rb`) walks it and writes one entry per Fachinfo **and date**, linking to the diff rather than to the Fachinfo. One entry per Fachinfo, not per registration: a Fachinfo belongs to 2.3 registrations on average and the text changed once, not twice. `jobs/build_fachinfo_year_feeds` is the one-off (dry run by default, `--apply` writes); `jobs/update_fachinfo_rss_feeds` uses the same class nightly at 05:01 in place of `update_yearly_fachinfo_feeds`. Tests: `test/test_util/fachinfo_year_feeds.rb`.
- **Reading the whole graph needs `odba_retire`, or the process eats the database.** A `FachinfoDocument` carries the entire text and a `ChangeLogItem` carries the old one *and* the new one — that double storage is why the database is 19 GB. Walking 1500 Fachinfos without releasing them stood at **2.7 GB and rising linearly**; all 6287 would have passed ten. Neither `ODBA.cache.clean` nor nulling the ivar helps, because the app's own `@fachinfos` hash and the memoized `@sorted_fachinfos` array hold every object alive. `ODBA.cache.fetch_cache_entry(id)&.odba_retire(force: true)` replaces the object with its Stub **in everything that holds it** — what ODBA itself does under memory pressure, and it writes nothing. Flat at 280 MB afterwards.
- **The English feed has always carried the German set.** There is no English Fachinfo document; `LookandfeelBase::DICTIONARIES` has de/fr/en and `l10n_sessions` writes three files from one model list. `FachinfoYearFeeds::LANGUAGES` therefore maps `en => de`. Italian documents exist and have their own change logs, but there is no `data/rss/it` and never was.
- **The yearly files stop carrying the full text**, which is the point and the cost: `fachinfo-2026.rss` was 231 MB because every item embedded a rendered Fachinfo. The main `fachinfo.rss` still does. A yearly file is now a list of changes with links, so it is small enough that `RssReader` no longer matters there.
